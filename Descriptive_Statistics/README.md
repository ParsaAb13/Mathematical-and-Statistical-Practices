## 📌 Section 1: Overview & Theoretical Foundations

Descriptive statistics are essential for understanding the underlying structure of data, detecting anomalies, and summarizing key data characteristics before moving to inferential modeling.

### Core Concepts Covered
1. **Measures of Central Tendency:**
   * **Mean ($\mu, \bar{x}$):** Arithmetic average of the dataset.
   * **Median:** The middle value in a sorted dataset, robust to outliers.
   * **Mode:** The most frequently occurring value(s).

2. **Measures of Dispersion & Variability:**
   * **Range:** Difference between the maximum and minimum values.
   * **Variance ($\sigma^2, s^2$):** Measure of how spread out data values are around the mean.
   * **Standard Deviation ($\sigma, s$):** Square root of the variance, expressed in original data units.
   * **Interquartile Range (IQR):** Difference between the 75th ($Q_3$) and 25th ($Q_1$) percentiles.

3. **Distribution Shape & Outlier Detection:**
   * **Skewness:** Measure of asymmetry of the probability distribution.
   * **Histogram and Scatter Plot:** Visualizations used to explore data distributions and relationship between variables.
   * **Boxplot Analysis :** Techniques used to identify statistical outliers.
